Material flags

Two readings that look alike on a bad day and go to different people. A place that has been asking for material, and a carrier whose count ShinGo may no longer be entitled to. They are selected on different columns, they are listed separately, and there is no combined number — reading them as one is what used to send every deep negative to the wrong owner.

Neither section attributes anything, and neither is a downtime figure. ShinGo records that a place asked for material and when it stopped asking. It does not record whether a line was stopped, whether anybody was waiting, or what it cost — so nothing here is a total of minutes, and nothing here should become one. A count of what is open right now cannot be accumulated into a scorecard; a sum of durations can, which is why there isn't one.

A negative ledger is a cycle count, not an outage. It means more parts went into the carrier than were declared, or parts arrived off the books. It does not suppress replenishment and must not: the reading is too low, so the honest response is to keep ordering and get the carrier counted.

Confidence: the binding age is exact where it is shown, and a dash means the audit trail holds no load, clear or release for that carrier — an age nobody knows, not a new binding. Whether a long binding has actually drifted is what the count tells you; this page only says which counts have had the longest to.

Why the ledger does not select these rows. Measured at Springfield over 531 bindings: the longest binding on record — 22d 23h — carried a ledger only 0.2× of one binload below zero, so a depth filter would have missed it, and the deepest ledger on record (10.2× of a binload) sat on a binding 1.6 hours old, so a depth filter would have flagged it. 166 of 191 negative bindings lasted under three days. The line at {{.OverpackBinloads}} of a carrier's own capacity is where one overpack stops explaining the number — it classifies the reading and selects nothing.
